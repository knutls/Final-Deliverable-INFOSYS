\subsection{Models}
\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Images/main_as-is.drawio.pdf}
    \caption{AS-IS main process at NTNU}
    \label{fig:main_as-is}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:main_as-is} presents a high-level BPMN model of the current (AS-IS) learning process at NTNU. The model consists of three pools: \textit{Student}, \textit{NTNU} and \textit{External sensor}. The NTNU pool is divided into lanes representing the systems and actors involved in the process: Studentweb/FS, Canvas, Panopto, Teaching Staff (lecturers and teaching assistants) and Inspera Assessment. The external sensor is modelled as a black-box pool. 

The process starts at the beginning of the semester, when the student selects courses and registers for them in Studentweb. The registration is sent to NTNU, where the student is registered on the courses in FS and subsequently given access to the corresponding course rooms in Canvas. Once the student receives access to the course rooms, the learning activities of the semester begin.

The learning phase is modelled as two parallel tracks. In the lecture track, the teaching staff deliver lectures, which are recorded and published in Canvas through Panopto. A student may attend the lecture in person, review the recording in Canvas, or do both. In the exercise track, the teaching staff publish course material and exercises in Canvas. The student works on the exercises and submits them in Canvas, after which the teaching staff assess the submissions and return feedback to the student.

When both tracks are completed, the student takes a digital exam in Inspera Assessment. The exam answers are made available to an external sensor, who grades the exam and returns the result to NTNU. The teaching staff receive and publish the grades, and the student is notified of the final grade through Studentweb, which concludes the process.

The model is purposely kept at a high level of abstraction. Only three activities are modelled as collapsed sub-processes, as these represent the focus areas of this project: course selection and registration (Case~3), recording and publishing of lecture recordings (Case~1), and assessment of exercises and feedback (Case~2). These sub-processes are elaborated in more detail in the following sections, while the remaining activities are included to provide context for the overall process.

\subsection{Cases}
\subsubsection{Case 1 - Lecture content is not searchable or reusable}
\textbf{Perceived problem:} Lecture recordings at NTNU (delivered via Panopto/Canvas) are stored as raw video with no transcription, summarization, or indexing. Students who want to review a specific concept must scrub through full recordings rather than search for it, and there is no automatically generated study material (summaries, key-point extraction, quiz questions) derived from lecture content. This underuses the opportunity for GenAI-based transcription and summarization highlighted in the case description, and creates inefficiency in how students prepare for exercises and exams.

\textbf{Model description:} Figure~\ref{fig:case1} expands the two collapsed subprocesses related to lecture recordings in the main model (Figure~\ref{fig:main_as-is}). To ensure that each subprocess belongs to a single lane, the handling of lecture recordings is split into two parts: (a) \textit{Record and store lecture recordings}, placed in the Panopto lane, and (b) \textit{Review lecture recordings in Canvas}, placed in the Student pool. The activities performed by the teaching staff and Canvas, ordering the recording and making it available in the Canvas course room, are shown in the main model. Identified pain points are marked as red annotations in the model, and the activities they concern are marked red.

Subprocess (a) starts after the lecturer has ordered a Panopto recording in the main model. Panopto first schedules an automated recording, and the process then waits at the timer event \textit{Scheduled lecture time reached}. When the lecture starts, Panopto records camera, audio and screen. The output is the data object \textit{Lecture recording}. The recording is then uploaded and stored in the course folder in the \textit{Panopto database}, and the subprocess ends with \textit{Recording stored}. All activities are modelled as service tasks, since Panopto performs them without human involvement once the recording has been ordered. Back in the main model, Canvas makes the recording available in the course room.

Subprocess (b) starts when the recording is available in Canvas. The student opens the recording in the Canvas course room. The next activity, \textit{Scrub through full recording to find concept}, is modelled as a looping user task. Since the recording has no transcript or topic-based index, the student has to jump back and forth in the video until the relevant part is found. Finally, the student writes their own notes and study material. This is modelled as a manual task, since it happens outside the systems. The subprocess ends with \textit{Lecture content reviewed}.

The model highlights three pain points. First, Panopto only stores the raw video. No activity processes the content of the recording, so there is no transcript, topic-based index, summary or study material. Panopto does generate a table of contents, but it is based on slide titles rather than topics. Second, because of this, the student must search for concepts by scrubbing through the video. Third, every student repeats the same work of reviewing the lecture and making notes, for every lecture.

\subsubsection{Case 2 - Feedback on exercises and assignments is minimal
}\textbf{Perceived problem}: Feedback on student exercises and assignments (via Canvas/Inspera)
is often limited to a numeric grade or a short comment, due to TA workload. Students receive
little actionable guidance on how to improve their writing, reasoning, or technical solutions,
which works against the case description's stated goal of "assisting the development of
students' writing skills and critical thinking/reflecting skills." There is no systematic
mechanism for scaling detailed, individualized feedback across large course cohorts.

\textbf{Model description:} Figure~\ref{fig:case2} expands the collapsed subprocess \textit{Assess exercises and give feedback} from the main model (Figure~\ref{fig:main_as-is}). The subprocess is placed in the Teaching Staff lane, and the expanded model therefore only shows the activities performed by the teaching staff. The activities performed by Canvas and the student are shown in the main model. Canvas receives the exercise submissions, and when the submission deadline has passed, it locks the assignment and collects the submissions. This starts the subprocess. Identified pain points are marked as red annotations in the model, and the activities they concern are marked red.

The subprocess starts when the submissions have been collected, and the teaching staff distribute them among the teaching assistants (TAs). The assessment is modelled as a loop that is repeated for each submission. The TA opens a submission and checks the answer against the solution proposal, which already exists and is shown as a data object in the model. The TA then registers a score or pass/fail status, which is stored in the Canvas gradebook. Next, an exclusive gateway represents the decision of whether there is sufficient time to make a comment. If so, the TA writes a short comment. If not, the comment is skipped, and the two paths merge again. A second exclusive gateway checks whether there are more submissions to assess. If there are, the flow returns to the start of the loop and the next submission is opened. When all submissions have been assessed, the subprocess ends with \textit{All submissions assessed}.

Back in the main model (Figure~\ref{fig:main_as-is}), Canvas publishes the scores and comments to the students, which sends the feedback to the student. In the Student pool, the student receives the feedback and reads the score and comment in Canvas before the exercise track continues towards the exam.

The model highlights three pain points. First, each submission is opened, checked and scored manually by a TA. The workload therefore grows with the size of the cohort, and there is no system support for scaling detailed, individual feedback. Second, whether a written comment is given depends on the time available to the TA, not on the needs of the student, so many students receive only a score. Third, when a comment is written, it is often short and generic, with little actionable guidance on writing, reasoning or the technical solution. The problem is therefore not that the TAs put too little effort into the feedback, but that the number of submissions leaves little time for each student. Students who want more detailed feedback must contact the teaching staff on their own initiative. These pain points are the main targets for the TO-BE solution presented in Section~4.2.


\subsubsection{Case 3 - Course selection and study plan personalization in Studentweb is static}
\label{sec:case3-asis}
\textbf{Perceived problem}: Studentweb lets students register for courses within a fixed program
structure, but offers no personalization based on individual interests, career goals, or prior
performance. Students choosing electives or specialization tracks must research options
manually across course catalogs and Innsida, with no system-supported recommendation of
which courses fit their profile or how to structure their remaining study plan. Many studyprogrammes at NTNU let students choose several electives courses, which gives them opportunity to shape their education towards the field they want to work in. Without guidance, however, it's difficult to know which courses fit a particular interest, As a result, students may choose courses that are not relevant to their goals, or miss courses they would have found more interesting. This leaves unrealized the case description's interest in personalized learning paths and curriculum optimization, instead of an adaptive selection process, students face a static list of options with no guidance layered on top

Figure~\ref{fig:case3} expands the collapsed subprocess "Select courses" from the main model (Figure~\ref{fig:asis-main}). The model consists of two pools: Student and NTNU. The Student pool contains the subprocess itself, while NTNU is shown as a black-box pool, since the student only communicates with the study advisor through messages. The advisor's own work is shown in the Study advisor lane of the main model. Identified pain points are marked as annotations in the model.

The process starts in the Student pool when course registration is needed. The student logs in to Studentweb using Feide and reviews their education plan and required courses. An exclusive gateway then represents whether the student has to choose elective courses. If not, the subprocess ends directly, and the student continues to registration in the main model (Figure~\ref{fig:asis-main}. If elective courses must be chosen, the student researches course options manually, using the NTNU course pages and Innsida as information sources, and compares the options against their own interests and goals. As highlighted in the model, there is no personalized recommendation at this stage, and the student must search and compare across several separate sources on their own.

After comparing the options, the student decides whether they need advice. If so, the student contacts a study advisor by sending a question to NTNU. The advisor then gives guidance on the course options and sends the advice back, which is shown in the "Study advisor" lane of the main model (Figure~\ref{fig:asis-main}). On the student side, an event-based gateway represents the two possible outcomes. Either the advice is received, or no answer has arrived within a given number of days. The length of this waiting period is subjective and depends on how long each student is able to wait. If the advice is received, the student decides whether further clarification is needed. If needed, the study advisor is contacted again. The model highlights that this guidance depends on the student taking the initiative, is not supported by any system, and involves an unknown waiting time before an answer is given.

When the student has either received sufficient advice, chosen not to wait any longer, or did not need advice in the first place, an exclusive gateway checks whether a suitable course has been found. If not, the student returns to researching course options manually. If suitable course is found the subprocess ends with "Courses selected", and the student continues to registration in Studentweb, which is shown in the main model (Figure~\ref{fig:asis-main})

The model shows that course selection is largely manual and driven by the student. In the overall process, system support is limited to validating and storing the registration in Studentweb and FS, as shown in the main model (Figure~\ref{fig:asis-main}). The step where the student has to decide what to choose, and the only point where personal guidance is available, depends entirely on the student’s own research and on the study advisor’s availability.
